You do not need to code. You do not need a mathematical background. Why This Collection Exists

AI should not be a black box reserved for technologists.

Artificial intelligence is quickly becoming part of how organizations remember, analyze, coordinate, invest, manage risk, and act. That makes AI literacy a business and civic capability—not a specialist luxury. These monographs translate the architecture of intelligent systems into plain language while preserving the seriousness of the underlying ideas.

The Central Idea

The model is not the system.

An AI becomes institutionally consequential when it is connected to knowledge, tools, memory, objectives, permissions, other agents, and the external world. Understanding those connections is the beginning of both value and governance.
Models provide capabilityThey interpret, generate, predict, classify, reason, or recognize patterns.
Architecture provides functionTools, workflows, memory, and orchestration turn capability into useful work.
Institutions provide purposeKnowledge, roles, mandates, and incentives determine what the system is trying to accomplish.
Governance provides legitimacyEvidence, permissions, accountability, monitoring, and reversibility determine what the system may do.

Honorary Fellow and External Lecturer at Cambridge Judge Business School; Independent Board Member at BIVA; Founder and Chief Scientist of DEFI Capital Research; financial-markets practitioner, researcher, and educator.
